Limit curves of deformations of Barbot representations

A cyclic Higgs bundle on the closed surface \(y^2 = x^5 - x\) gives a representation of its fundamental group in \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\). When the cubic term \(t\) vanishes, it is a Barbot representation, and its limit curve is a projective line. A nonzero \(t\) deforms the representation and bends the line. Choose \(t\) and watch the curve, drawn on the sphere \(S^2\) that double covers \(\mathbb{RP}^2\).

What is drawn

Attracting eigenlines

Let \(\rho\colon \pi_1 S \to \mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\) be the holonomy. When \(\rho(\gamma)\) has a simple real eigenvalue of largest modulus, its eigenline \(p_\gamma \in \mathbb{RP}^2\) is the attracting fixed point of \(\rho(\gamma)\). For an Anosov representation, these points lie on the limit curve \(\xi(\partial \pi_1 S)\) and are dense in it.

The page computes \(p_\gamma\) for the cyclically reduced words of length at most \(3\) in \(a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2\), then moves these points by the generators, since

\[\rho(g)\, p_\gamma = p_{g\gamma g^{-1}}.\]

Each point of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) has two unit lifts \(\pm p \in S^2\), and both are drawn: green on the front hemisphere, violet behind. In the affine chart \(X_j \neq 0\), the point \([X_0 : X_1 : X_2]\) has coordinates \(X_k / X_j\).

How it is computed

From the Higgs bundle to the holonomy

On \(X\colon y^2 = x^5 - x\), let \(S = \mathcal O(p_\infty)\) be the odd square root of \(K\), with a section \(s\) such that \(s^2 = dx/y\). The Higgs bundle is

\[E = S \oplus \mathcal O \oplus S^{-1}, \qquad \Phi = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & t & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & t \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad t \in H^0\big(X, K^{3/2}\big).\]

For \(t = 0\), the representation is the Fuchsian representation in \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\) acting on \(S \oplus S^{-1}\), extended trivially on \(\mathcal O\): a Barbot representation.

The page solves for the harmonic metric \(H = \operatorname{diag}(h, 1, h^{-1})\) on two consecutive meshes of \(X\) and extrapolates, then integrates the flat connection \(\nabla_H + \Phi + \Phi^{*_H}\) along loops representing \(a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2\). Everything runs in this browser, in Python (Pyodide, NumPy, SciPy); nothing is sent to a server.

The relation error of the computed matrices is

\[\big\| [\rho(a_1), \rho(b_1)]\,[\rho(a_2), \rho(b_2)] - I \big\|_F .\]

The picture is a finite sample computed from approximate holonomies, not a certified curve; more points do not make the holonomy more accurate.
