Limit curves of deformations of Barbot representations

A cyclic Higgs bundle on the closed surface \(y^2 = x^5 - x\) gives a representation of its fundamental group in \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\). When the cubic term \(t\) vanishes, it is a Barbot representation, and its limit curve is a projective line. As \(t = A\,s^3\) grows, the representation is deformed and the line bends into a more and more polygonal curve. Move \(A\) and watch the curve, drawn on the sphere \(S^2\) that double covers \(\mathbb{RP}^2\).

PaperAnosov deformations of Barbot representations, with Samuel Bronstein · arXiv:2502.09107

What is drawn

Attracting eigenlines

Let \(\rho\colon \pi_1 S \to \mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\) be the holonomy. The attracting eigenlines \(p_\gamma \in \mathbb{RP}^2\) of the matrices \(\rho(\gamma)\) lie on the limit curve and are dense in it. The words beginning with a finite word \(g\) give the points \(\xi(g\,\ell\, m\, m \cdots) = \rho(g\ell m)\, p_m\); a piece of the curve whose points spread over more than \(0.1^\circ\) is refined with longer words, so the sampling follows the curve where its points are still far apart.

The harmonic metrics and holonomies were computed in advance, in Python; each picture is a finite sample from approximate holonomies, not a certified curve.
