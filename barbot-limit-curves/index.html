Limit curves of deformations of Barbot representations

A cyclic Higgs bundle on the closed surface \(y^2 = x^5 - x\) gives a representation of its fundamental group in \(\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\). When the cubic term \(t\) vanishes, it is a Barbot representation, and its limit curve is a projective line. As \(t = A\,s^3\) grows, the representation is deformed and the line bends into a more and more polygonal curve. Move \(A\) and watch the curve, drawn on the sphere \(S^2\) that double covers \(\mathbb{RP}^2\).

PaperAnosov deformations of Barbot representations, with Samuel Bronstein · arXiv:2502.09107

What is drawn

Attracting eigenlines

Let \(\rho\colon \pi_1 S \to \mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb R)\) be the holonomy. When \(\rho(\gamma)\) has a simple real eigenvalue of largest modulus, its eigenline \(p_\gamma \in \mathbb{RP}^2\) is the attracting fixed point of \(\rho(\gamma)\). For an Anosov representation, these points lie on the limit curve \(\xi(\partial \pi_1 S)\) and are dense in it.

Every point of the limit curve is \(\xi(w)\) for an infinite reduced word \(w\) in \(a_1^{\pm1}, b_1^{\pm1}, a_2^{\pm1}, b_2^{\pm1}\). The words beginning with a finite word \(g\) form a piece of the boundary, whose image on the curve contains the points

\[\xi(g\,\ell\, m\, m\, m \cdots) = \rho(g\ell m)\, p_m .\]

The sampling is adaptive: a piece whose points spread over more than \(0.1^\circ\) in \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is cut into longer words, the widest pieces first, so the curve is explored where its points are still far apart. Words never contain more than half of the relator \([a_1, b_1][a_2, b_2]\), so that no element is reached through the relation, which the computed matrices satisfy only approximately.

Each point of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) has two unit lifts \(\pm p \in S^2\), and both are drawn: green on the front hemisphere, violet behind. In the affine chart \(X_j \neq 0\), the point \([X_0 : X_1 : X_2]\) has coordinates \(X_k / X_j\).

How it is computed

From the Higgs bundle to the holonomy

On \(X\colon y^2 = x^5 - x\), let \(S = \mathcal O(p_\infty)\) be the odd square root of \(K\), with a section \(s\) such that \(s^2 = dx/y\). The Higgs bundle is

\[E = S \oplus \mathcal O \oplus S^{-1}, \qquad \Phi = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & t & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & t \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad t \in H^0\big(X, K^{3/2}\big).\]

For \(t = 0\), the representation is the Fuchsian representation in \(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)\) acting on \(S \oplus S^{-1}\), extended trivially on \(\mathcal O\): a Barbot representation.

For each value of \(A\), the harmonic metric \(H = \operatorname{diag}(h, 1, h^{-1})\) is solved on three successively refined meshes of \(X\), the two finest solutions are combined by Richardson extrapolation, and the flat connection \(\nabla_H + \Phi + \Phi^{*_H}\) is integrated along loops representing \(a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2\). These computations were done in advance, in Python; the page only displays their results.

The accuracy shown is the largest relative change of a generator \(\rho(a_i), \rho(b_i)\) between the two finest meshes. The relation error \(\big\| [\rho(a_1), \rho(b_1)]\,[\rho(a_2), \rho(b_2)] - I \big\|_F\) is recorded too, but it grows with the size of the matrices and is not comparable across values of \(A\).

Each picture is a finite sample computed from approximate holonomies, not a certified curve. The slider stops at \(A = 1\): beyond, the computed holonomy of \(b_1\) is no longer stable under mesh refinement (it changes by \(16\,\%\) at \(A = 1.5\)).
