Barbot construction and limit map

For a nonzero monic polynomial \(t\), we use

\[ \Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&t&0\\0&0&t\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}dz, \qquad H=\operatorname{diag}(h,1,h^{-1}). \]

Writing \(w=-2\log h\), the metric equation and its asymptotics are

\[ \Delta w=8e^w-8|t|^2e^{-w/2},\qquad w\sim\frac43\log|t|,\qquad g=e^w|dz|^2. \]

For \(t\equiv1\), we have \(h\equiv1\). In an ordered, \(H\)-orthonormal eigenbasis of \(\psi_0(v)\), the cone is \(x_1^2\ge x_2^2+x_3^2\). Its distinguished centre is the positive eigendirection. If \(P_\gamma(L)\) transports vectors from the origin to the endpoint, the plotted approximation to the limit map is

\[ \xi_L(\theta)=\bigl[P_{\gamma_\theta}(L)^{-1}c_+(\dot\gamma_\theta(L))\bigr] \in\mathbb{RP}^2. \]

The right picture shows only metric-geodesic samples. Consecutive samples are joined by a projective segment. Neighbours on the same projective line determine its direction; tightly clustered vertex samples use a short segment. Ambiguous sparse cases use the continuous homogeneous lifts supplied by transport. Across the closing seam these lifts are antipodal. This is a reconstruction heuristic: increase the number of rays when segment choices remain uncertain.

For chosen lifts \(p,q\), the segment is \([(1-s)p+sq]\), \(0\le s\le1\). If its chart denominator changes sign, we split it at infinity and clip each visible piece separately. No interior is filled. Switch affine charts, adjust the view, or turn segments off to inspect the original samples.

The slider uses Euclidean path length \(L\); the curves are reparametrized geodesics of \(g\), with metric arclength exported separately. Cones are nested along metric geodesics, and only asymptotically nested along straight rays. Straight rays remain available in the domain comparison only. Polygonality is work in progress; these finite-cutoff samples are not a certified limiting curve.

Equations, accuracy & reading the picture

The reference construction

For \(C=q(z)\,dz^3\), the Blaschke metric \(g=e^w|dz|^2\) satisfies

\[\Delta w=2e^w-4|q|^2e^{-2w}.\]

We integrate the real affine-sphere frame along equally spaced straight rays from \(z=0\). A degree-\(d\) polynomial gives a convex polygon with \(d+3\) vertices.

For \(q\equiv1\), \(w=\frac13\log2\) exactly. The limiting triangle has circumradius \(\sqrt2\) in this normalization.

Finite computations

The square-domain boundary data are \(w=\frac13\log(2|q|^2)\). The orange polygon joins finite-cutoff samples on \(d+3\) asymptotic sector-centre rays. The teal curve joins uniformly sampled rays at the selected radius.

Increase grid resolution, domain, and cutoff to check stability. Vertex drift measures radius change only, not total numerical error. The initial frame is fixed, and ill-conditioned full frames are never inverted.
